What We Offer

Services built around your comfort

Every option below is explained in plain language before we touch a tooth — what it fixes, what it feels like, and why it's the right call for your case.

Tooth-Colored Fillings

Composite resin fillings replace the old metal kind. They're matched to your natural tooth shade, bond directly to the tooth, and require removing less healthy structure than a traditional silver filling — which can actually weaken a back tooth over time.

Inlays & Onlays

For a cavity too large for a filling but not large enough to need a full crown, a lab-made inlay or onlay fits the gap precisely. It preserves more of your natural tooth, adds strength where you need it, and blends in well enough that it's hard to spot once placed.

Crowns & Bridges

A crown ("cap") covers a tooth with a large cavity, a fracture risk, or one that's had root canal therapy — in all-ceramic, porcelain-fused-to-metal, or all-metal, depending on the tooth and your preference. A bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring to the teeth on either side. Either way, plan on two visits: the first for preparation, a mold, and a temporary; the second to seat the final piece.

Porcelain Veneers

Ultra-thin porcelain facings that mask staining, hide chips, correct minor misalignment, and even out a smile's shape — without braces. Veneers can also brighten discolored teeth, repair fractures, close small gaps, and lengthen worn teeth, while conserving far more tooth structure than a crown would.

Dental Implants

A titanium post replaces the root of a missing tooth and bonds to the surrounding bone (osseointegration). Once it's secure, a lab-made crown is attached on top. An implant replaces a tooth without touching the teeth next to it, and can also help stabilize a denture.

Zoom Whitening

A professional in-office treatment using Zoom! Advanced Power gel and a blue-plasma light, typically lifting your shade six to ten levels in about an hour — followed by a quick five-minute fluoride treatment to finish.

NTI Night Guards

An FDA-approved device worn over the upper or lower front two teeth during sleep. It's used for migraine prevention, tension-type headaches, bruxism (teeth grinding), and TMJ symptoms — and it protects your teeth from chips, cracks, and wear in the meantime.

Not sure which of these you need?

That's exactly what the initial visit is for. Tell us what's bothering you and we'll walk you through the right option.
